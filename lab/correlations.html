Correlation, dispersion and the cost of luck

Everything on this page is computed from official NFL records committed under data/lab/: player game logs for the 2026 regular season and the official Game Book for Bills at Vikings on November 9, 2026. These are properties of the game data. They are not probabilities of winning, they are not prices, and they are not evidence of an edge.

Sample sizes are small and the intervals are wide. Every correlation below rests on 6 to 8 matched games. They are published with their intervals precisely so that nobody reads a point estimate as a fact. The purpose is to show that the legs of a parlay are not independent, and to set the sample size that any future claim has to meet.

The two-receiver pair is the important one. r = -0.82 on six matched games, with an interval of -0.98 to -0.02. The point estimate says the two receivers' yardage moved in opposite directions, which is what a target split looks like. The interval is wide and only just excludes zero, so this is a demonstration of the structure rather than a usable estimate. Either way, independence is the wrong model, and the direction of the error favours the book.

Method and limits

  • Inputs: data/lab/player-logs-2026.json and data/lab/gamebook-bills-vikings-2026-w9.json; the code is scripts/analyze_lab.py and is re-run in CI.
  • Every Week 9 row was cross-checked against the official Game Book PDF and matched exactly on attempts, completions, yards, touchdowns, interceptions, rating and rushing.
  • Only the 2026 season was transcribed, and only for five players on two teams. A production system needs several seasons and a much wider player set before any of these coefficients could be traded on.
  • No price, no settlement and no forecast record exists here, so nothing on this page is an expected value, a yield or a fair price.
