Model outputs, not official values, and not betting advice. Every number on these pages is computed from official NFL.com results, official box scores and official club depth charts. The originated lines, probabilities, returns and rewards are this repository's model output - they are not published by the league and they are not a market price. The reference price of -110 per leg is a declared convention used so strategies can be compared with each other. No claim is made that any strategy beats a real market. See Validation and Questions.

Questions, information and answers

Before a decision can reasonably be made, the question has to be stated, what is already known has to be separated from what is not, and a concrete step for finding the missing piece has to exist. That is what this register is for.

The blocking questions are the honest limit of this work. 8 questions must be answered before any claim about market edge, injury-driven value, prop settlement, or rewards can be made. They are listed first in the table below so they are not buried.

QN-01Which market are our lines intended to be compared against?NFL.com publishes the schedule, results, box scores and injury reports. It does not publish betting prices. Every price in this lab is therefore a declared reference price, not a market quote.Any official, licenseable sportsbook price feed. No league source exists for it.Decide the market universe (spread, total, team total, player props) and the book set, then obtain a licensed price feed and archive timestamped quotes. Until then, no strategy in this lab can be described as beating a market.official
QN-02What exactly does a strategy's probability mean, and is it calibrated?Every strategy publishes its own probability for every bet, and the leaderboard reports log loss, Brier score and expected calibration error against the realised outcome.Calibration judged on data that was never used to fit anything. The 2026 season is the untouched holdout, so this is partly answered already.Re-run the same pipeline at the end of the 2026 season and read the holdout calibration; keep the freeze date in the repository.official
QN-03How much of a strategy's result is luck rather than skill?Gate G6 refuses a strategy whose profit is concentrated in one bet, and G4 requires a bootstrap interval that excludes zero after multiple-testing correction.A longer untouched period. Two seasons of development and one holdout season is a small sample for parlay strategies in particular.Extend the untouched holdout across the remainder of the 2026 season and the 2027 season, with the freeze date published in advance.official
QN-04Where is the official historical archive of weekly injury reports?NFL.com publishes the current week's injury report, with player, position, injury, practice status and game status.Any week-by-week history from an official source. The current-week page ignores a week parameter, so past weeks cannot be read from it.Ask the NFL communications contact for the official weekly injury report archive, or determine whether the league-published weekly game statistics books carry the status lists. Both are official routes; neither is available from the pages we can reach today.official
QN-05Are the club depth charts official or unofficial, and are they point-in-time?Each club publishes a depth chart on its own official site. We capture the club's own label and the 'as of' date.A history of past depth charts. We hold only the current version, so a depth chart can only inform today's tracker, not a backtest.Ask each club whether prior-week depth charts are archived; in the meantime treat every depth-derived historical statement as unverified.official
QN-06What is the correct home-field advantage?The model uses a fixed 3.0 point home-field term, declared on the validation page.An official value. No league publishes home-field advantage, and it is not a fact we can verify - it is a modelling assumption.Treat it as a declared parameter, publish a sensitivity table over 2.0 to 4.0 points, and refuse to publish a strategy whose gates only pass at one value.official
QN-07Which official source resolves a player name to a stable identifier?The NFL.com box score identifies each player by an official image id and shows a display form such as 'A. ST. BROWN'. Club depth charts give the full name.A direct official name-to-id mapping. We join the two through the display form, which is ambiguous for 156 display forms where two charted players share initials.Use the official roster page of each club as the third join, or accept the ambiguous rows as permanently unverified and exclude them from published props.official
QN-08How should a player prop line be set when the player is a listed backup rather than the starter?The official depth chart gives the tier (1st to 4th string) for every charted player.An official rule linking tier to expected workload. No league source states that a second-string player receives X percent of the carries.Derive the tier-to-workload relationship from official box scores (minutes, attempts and targets by depth-chart tier), then publish the measured table instead of an assumed multiplier.official
QN-09Does a team's injury actually change its line, and by how much?The official injury report and the official depth chart together identify which starter is replaced by which tier.The size of the effect. This requires the historical injury archive from QN-04.Once QN-04 is answered, measure the change in team scoring and total points when a starter is replaced, and publish the measured effect with its sample size.official
QN-10How are player prop payouts settled when a player does not play?Nothing in any official source we can reach. Settlement rules belong to the bookmaker.The exact rules (void, half action, minimum participation) that would be needed to score a prop bet.Obtain the rule set for each book in scope before any prop strategy is priced. Until then, prop results in this lab use the model's own over/under rule and say so.official
QN-11What is the correct correlation to use between parlay legs?We measure the correlation from official per-game outcomes and publish the value with its sample size and confidence interval.A correlation that is stable across seasons and situations. The within-game prop correlation is small, but the estimate pools every player pair.Split the measurement by player pair type (same player, same position, different position) and require a minimum sample per cell before any cell is used for pricing.official
QN-12Should the leaderboard reward points, money, or something else?A declared, published formula turns out-of-sample skill into leaderboard points.Any governance decision: reward pool, eligibility, privacy, responsible-play controls, anti-gaming rules, legal review.Owner decision plus legal and responsible-play review before the leaderboard is used for anything other than a paper exercise.official
QN-13Which teams and games should the tracker watch next week?The official schedule publishes the full 2026 regular season, and the official injury report publishes this week's status for every game.Nothing for this week. The tracker is limited to the current week's report because that is the only week the league publishes.Re-run the injury fetch each week and store each week's snapshot so a history builds up from the first run.official
QN-14Is the model better than a coin flip, or just better than a bad baseline?Every strategy is scored against a pre-registered naive model on the same events, and the log-loss difference is published with a confidence interval.A comparison against a strong public baseline such as a market-implied probability, because no market price is available.Same as QN-01: a licensed price feed. Until then, 'beats the naive model' is the strongest claim the evidence supports.official
QN-15Do the two NFL datasets inside this repository agree with each other?The planner already holds an official NFL 2026 feed, and this lab holds an independently fetched one.A published side-by-side reconciliation between the two feeds.Compare the two committed feeds game by game and publish the count of agreements and differences.official
QN-16What is the official definition of a starter for the depth tracker?Club depth charts order players by tier (1st, 2nd, 3rd, 4th string) per position group.A league-wide definition. Clubs disagree on granularity: some list a position twice (a first and a second tight end), others list eleven offensive positions only.Publish the position granularity used per club and treat any comparison across clubs as approximate.official
QN-17Should weather be part of the model?The official game page publishes the observed weather at kickoff.An official forecast at decision time, which is what a model would need. Observed weather cannot be used without leaking the outcome.Either locate an official forecast source usable at decision time or leave weather out of the model and state that omission as an evidence limit.official
QN-18How should trades, promotions and roster moves be handled?NFL.com publishes an official transactions feed.A parsed, dated transaction history joined to the player identity used by this lab.Fetch and parse the official transactions feed, then join by the player image id so a mid-season starter change is visible in the tracker.official
QN-19Are the club depth charts we publish complete for all 32 clubs?31 of 32 clubs publish a machine-readable depth chart on their official site.The Houston Texans depth chart, whose page is rendered without a readable table.Open a request with the club for an accessible version, or capture the chart image and treat the entries as unverified.official
QN-20Which reference price should the leaderboard use?-110 per leg is declared and used consistently for every strategy and every leg.A justification from evidence, because there is no official price to justify it against.Publish the leaderboard at several reference prices and report whether the ranking is stable. If the order changes, the ranking is a price artefact and must be labelled as such.official
QN-21How many seasons of official box scores can we rely on for player props?Official box scores were fetched for the 2025 and 2026 regular seasons.The 2024 season, which would give prop strategies a third season for the robustness gate.Extend the fetcher's season list and re-run; the official 2024 schedule and box scores are reachable.official
QN-22Do we need a freeze date before publishing a strategy ranking?The development and holdout split is declared in the published data.An explicit public freeze date and a rule for what happens when a strategy changes after the freeze.Owner decision: publish a freeze date with the leaderboard, and keep every rejected variant in the audit history rather than deleting it.official
QN-23Can the free-time planner and the lines lab share one NFL dataset?Both use official NFL.com data; the planner's feed covers 2026 and this lab's feed covers 2024 to 2026.A decision on which feed is canonical, so the two halves of the site cannot drift apart.Publish the reconciliation (QN-15) and then designate one feed as canonical, with the other generated from it.official
QN-24What is the acceptable standard for publishing a line at all?The current standard in this repository: every table row links an official source, and anything unconfirmed is blank and flagged.A written rule for model outputs. A model number is not an official value, so it needs its own labelling rule and its own provenance link back to the inputs.Owner decision: adopt the rule used here (model output labelled as such, with the official inputs linked) and apply it to the planner pages as well.official
