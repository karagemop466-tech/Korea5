How to represent backups

Replace the player, not a made-up “string penalty”

Offense

Quarterback / offensive line / skill group

For every offensive starter reported out or limited, associate an official dated depth-chart replacement where published; then measure the replacement's actual role and performance using historical appearances. Model unit interactions (e.g. QB × pass protection; line continuity × run outcomes) and mark unavailable fields missing.

Do not assume the second-listed player gets all lost usage. Report the unknown snap share and use scenarios or wider prediction intervals until verified.

Evidence inputs: NFL injury report · official player stats

Defense

Front seven / secondary / special teams

Record defensive starters and listed replacements by unit. Evaluate affected coverage / pass-rush / run-defense metrics against opponent and situation, accounting for snap share and teammates. A defender's absence may shift other players' roles; count exposure at unit level rather than assuming a one-for-one loss.

Call a defense “deficient” only when a declared metric is meaningfully worse than a preselected, opponent-adjusted baseline with uncertainty and sufficient sample support—not merely because a backup plays.

Evidence inputs: NFL defense passing · NFL defense rushing

Proposed lineup impact score (not calculated)

Use a versioned, position-specific estimate of lost expected contribution: replacement exposure × (starter expected contribution − replacement expected contribution), with teammate/role interactions and uncertainty. Values and coefficients stay blank until an official role source, observed participation data, historical sample, and calibration test exist. Avoid scoring solely from a depth-chart rank.

Measure the incremental forecast change when the lineup feature is added versus the same frozen model without it. This tests information value rather than merely associating injuries with losses.

Questions to resolve

Lineup-specific decision checklist

  1. Which clubs publish dated official depth charts, and can those pages be archived automatically?
  2. Can official NFL gamebooks / game pages provide reliable player participation or snap counts for every game and season?
  3. How should “limited” be represented without pretending to know a player's true availability probability?
  4. Which position-level performance metrics are predeclared, opponent-adjusted and stable enough to measure unit weakness?
  5. How will the data pipeline freeze the exact injury information known at each historical line timestamp to prevent leakage?
  6. Which source terms permit storing / redistributing full injury reports and historical odds snapshots?

These questions remain open; see the flagged register. The current source check confirmed the cited official pages exist, not a complete all-team depth-chart feed.
