Price math: from quoted odds to a fair probability

These are explicit calculation definitions for single markets and combinations. They do not generate a prediction, imply an advantage, validate a price source, or say randomness can be removed.

No current quote or fair price is published. The repository's 2026 model uses third-party market-line fields with a provenance flag; the visible market spread/total cells remain blank. No player-prop quotes or settled prop history are present. NFL.com Terms §1.3 is recorded as a source-rights warning; do not enable systematic collection without appropriate authorization.

1. American odds: raw implied probability

Plus odds

p_raw = 100 / (A + 100)

For American price +A, where A > 0.

Minus odds

p_raw = |A| / (|A| + 100)

For American price −A, where A > 0.

Decimal return

d = 1 + A/100

For plus odds. For negative American price −A, use d = 1 + 100/A. Decimal odds include the returned stake.

The raw implied probabilities include the bookmaker's margin (vig). For example, a quoted two-way market is not fair merely because each side's implied probability is calculated correctly.

2. One explicit no-vig method

p_fair(i) = p_raw(i) / Σ p_raw(j)

For mutually exclusive and exhaustive outcomes, this proportional method rescales the raw probabilities to sum to one. It is a convention, not the unique true probability estimator. Preserve the original quotes and compare sensitivity to other documented methods when an authorized complete market is available.

3. Fair decimal price and single-bet expected value

Model fair decimal price

d_fair = 1 / p_model

Only defined for 0 < p_model < 1. A model probability must be calibrated and timestamped; this equation does not validate it.

Net expected profit per unit

EV = p_win × (d − 1) − p_loss

Here d is the exact offered decimal price. A push has zero net profit; voids, limits and market-specific grading need explicit settlement rules.

For parlays, replace the single-event win probability with the probability of the complete joint settlement outcome and use the exact offered payout. Do not multiply leg probabilities by default.

4. Probability scoring and paired dependence

Brier score (binary event)

mean((p_i − y_i)²)

Lower is better; score only forecasts frozen before the outcome.

Binary log loss

−mean(y log p + (1−y) log(1−p))

Lower is better; extreme probabilities are strongly penalized.

Observed joint win probability

count(A win and B win) / paired N

Keep exact thresholds, settlement rules, event IDs and as-of times. This empirical frequency is not itself out-of-sample calibration.

Binary phi association

corr(1[A wins], 1[B wins])

A descriptive dependence statistic; a coefficient alone does not determine the payout probability of a multi-leg combination.

When observations share a game or week, uncertainty should preserve those clusters. Use predeclared chronological holdouts, show sample size and interval, and account for multiple tested variants. A high/low correlation estimate cannot erase variance in any individual game.
