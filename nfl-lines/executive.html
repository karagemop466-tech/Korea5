6. Known gaps — information that must be found before decisions

Each gap blocks a class of decision. Acquisition suggestions are listed; nothing is filled in by guesswork.

IDMissing informationWhy it blocks decisionsHow to obtain it
GAP-1Historical betting odds (opening and closing lines, all books)Without a timestamped line history, no closing-line value and no return on investment can be computed. Every 'edge' claim in the wider betting literature depends on this.Licensed historical odds feed (e.g. a commercial sports-data vendor) or a manually archived daily snapshot before every kickoff, stored with a timestamp.
GAP-2Player prop prices and settlement rulesProp combos need the joint price and the exact void/push rules per book.A prop market feed with timestamps, or an archived book screen per week.
GAP-3Official starting lineups at kickoff (inactives list)The injury report is a pre-game designation; the actual inactive list is the truth.ESPN summary endpoint inactives per game (endpoint reachable from CI; fetch job queued), or NFL.com game books.
GAP-4Player talent / value weightingThe lineup index weights players by snap share, which measures usage, not ability.A documented player-value model (e.g. published approximate value) or per-player EPA, both derivable from play-by-play once it is fetched in full.
GAP-5Referee and weather effects with adequate sampleOfficiating crews and weather are plausible signals but need thousands of games per subgroup.Already fetched; needs a dedicated gate with a minimum-subgroup size rule.
GAP-6Injury report timestamps precise enough for a decision cutoffA strategy is only usable if the information existed before the bet had to be placed.Use date_modified where present; otherwise fall back to the week boundary and label the row.

7. Open questions before any decision can be made

IDQuestion before any decisionInformation missingImpact
Q1Is the goal forecast accuracy against a baseline, or profit against a price?A timestamped price history to measure profit.Decides whether this lab is a research model or a betting system. Today it is the former.
Q2Which markets and which books are in scope?Book list, jurisdiction, prop market, settlement rules per book.Parlay pricing and prop combos are undefined without exact book rules.
Q3What is the decision cutoff for a strategy to count?A consistent cutoff before older seasons and a rule for late scratches.Without a cutoff a backtest can accidentally use information that did not exist yet.
Q4What may a top strategy actually win?Legal review before any money or prize of monetary value, and the tax/privacy handling that goes with it.Rewards are the incentive that turns a leaderboard into a competition.
Q5Do we need inactives (the actual game-day list), not just the injury report?The per-game inactive list in a machine-readable file.A 'Questionable' player often plays; an 'Out' player never does. Mixing them corrupts the lineup index.
Q6How should a missing starter be valued?A talent measure independent of usage.A team can lose a great player who had low usage and barely move the index.

8. What is claimed, and what is not

  • Claimed: the strength family (Elo-style ratings + form) beats its own baseline out of sample and survives the best-of-K luck-deflation null at the reported p95.
  • Not claimed: any profit, return, payout or edge. No timestamped price history exists (GAP-1), so none of those can be computed, and this lab is a research model, not a betting system.
  • Not claimed: that context-family skill is real — its deflation verdict is computed.
  • Injuries and depth charts are tracked with snapshot dates and a 1st–4th-string tier table (Injuries & lineups); designations come from the official weekly reports via nflverse.
