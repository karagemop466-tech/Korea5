Injuries & tiers

Two separate questions, kept separate: who is officially unavailable right now, and does losing a tier-1 player actually change the score. The first is official. The second is measured, and the measured answer is "not much".

Tiers are computed, not official. The NFL depth-chart pages return HTTP 404 from this environment (checked 2026-11-13). Rather than invent a depth order, tiers are ranked from last season's production within the same club and position group, and every row says so. A rookie or new signing with no production record gets a blank tier.

Does a tier-1 absence cost points?

This is the question that decides whether injury data can ever be a betting input. Answer measured on 414 club-weeks of the 2025 season, comparing club points with and without an officially unavailable tier-1 player.

Scope: three of the week's games. The remaining games are absent, not blank — a tracked coverage gap on the Flagged page.

Method and limitations

  • Tier-1 player for a club and position group = highest trailing 4-game production among players with a game in that group before the week in question.
  • An absence is counted only when the mirror records the official status Out, Doubtful or Injured Reserve for that club-week.
  • The comparison is a raw difference of means with a bootstrap interval. It is a measured association in one season, not a causal estimate, and it is not used as a betting input.
  • Offensive line is NOT included: the archive has no per-snap or per-start line data, so no line-breakdown claim is made.

Limitations

  • Injured Reserve is a season-long designation in the mirror, so a player on IR from week 1 is counted as unavailable for every later week.
  • The 2025 mirror carries no practice participation or primary injury, so severity cannot be graded.
  • Only one season of overlap exists in the reachable archive, so the sample is about one season of club-weeks.
  • Name matching is exact (club + full name). Unmatched players are dropped rather than fuzzy-matched.
