Tier definitions

Tier A

Passes sample size, positive out-of-sample skill, best-of-K deflation, calibration slope in [0.8, 1.25] and sign stability. Score is deflated skill × sample shrink × calibration factor.

Tier C

Positive out-of-sample skill but has not cleared deflation, or has no market to price against. Promising, not yet distinguishable from luck.

Tier D

Fails at least one gate. Kept in the audit history with the failing gate named — a failed strategy is never deleted, because deleting them is how selection bias starts.

Why win rate is not the ranking metric

A strategy can win 60% of 40 games through noise alone. Ranking on win rate rewards that. Ranking on deflated skill punishes it: the score subtracts the best-of-K null threshold, so a strategy must beat what the best of K random strategies would have scored on the same data before it earns anything.

Tie-break: higher deflated skill, then larger sample, then better calibration slope.
