Fair lines, and the price they imply

A fair line is the number at which the model thinks the bet is a coin flip. The price that follows from it — the break-even price — is the actual origination. Compare it with what the market asks and you can see the whole disagreement in one row.

No row can be labelled PLAY. A PLAY label requires a strategy that has passed every validation gate, including a frozen prospective trial and a human release review. None has, so the strongest label here is WATCH. This is deliberate: the site must not dress a research number as a pick.

Gap = model fair home margin minus the archive's number, in points. Positive means the model thinks the home club is stronger than the number does.

How each number is produced

  • Ratings: Elo with margin-weighted updates and season regression, built only from graded games.
  • Fair spread: an OLS mapping from (home win probability - 0.5) to the actual home margin, fitted on every graded game in the archive and reported in marginMapping.
  • Fair total: each club's points-for and points-allowed rate shrunk toward the league mean with a 6-game prior, averaged across both clubs.
  • Fair price: 1 / model probability, converted to American odds. It is the price at which the model thinks the bet is a coin flip - exactly what 'originating a line' means.

What is deliberately NOT in these numbers

  • Official depth-chart tiers: nfl.com depth-chart pages return 404 here, so injury/backup tier adjustment is NOT applied to these fair lines.
  • Player props: no prop prices exist in the archive, so no prop lines are originated.
  • Weather: not in the archive. Indoor/outdoor is known but conditions are not.

Did the model's number beat the market's number?

No. On 7,413 graded games the model's mean absolute error on the home margin is 10.52 points; the closing number's own error on the same games is 10.48 points. The market is better, and publishing that is the point. See the full comparison →
