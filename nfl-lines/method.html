Method

The rules this lab follows, so that every number can be audited or attacked.

The seven honesty rules

  1. No invented value. If an official source has not published it, the cell is blank and the row is on Flagged.
  2. Every row carries a source. Official where one exists; MIRROR where a documented public mirror was used; MODEL where the lab computed it; NOT OFFICIAL where no source can exist (market prices).
  3. Walk-forward only. A model for a game may use only games that kicked off before it. No random splits, no future information.
  4. No point estimate without an interval. Whole weeks are resampled, because games in a week share conditions.
  5. Correct for the family. Every p-value is multiplied by the number of strategies tested.
  6. Blocked is not zero. A strategy with no data shows BLOCKED and a blank profit. Zero would imply it was tested and lost.
  7. No reward without a prospective trial. Backtest evidence can never promote a strategy on its own.

Definitions

Signal

1 − (Brier score ÷ Brier score of always predicting the base rate). Zero means the forecast carries no information; 1 would be perfect. It is computed on the same games for the model and the market, so the two are comparable.

De-vigged probability

The implied probabilities of both sides of a price are normalised so they sum to one, removing the book's margin. Without this, any comparison against the market would be biased in the model's favour.

phi (φ)

The Pearson correlation of two 0/1 outcomes. It describes dependence; it is not a pricing formula. Pricing uses the observed joint probability.

Fair price

The reciprocal of the model probability, expressed as American odds. It is the price at which the model thinks the bet is a coin flip — hold-free, and therefore never an offer you can take.

Computed tier

Rank by the previous full season's production within the same club and position group. A player with no production record gets a blank tier. This is not an official depth chart.

One correction worth reading

An earlier build of this lab tested luck with a pooled break-even win rate: it assumed every bet needed the same win probability, taken as the average implied by the prices. That is wrong whenever prices differ. A win on a +900 long shot and a win on a -300 favourite count equally in a win count but not in the profit, so the test can declare significance that the money does not support.

The test was replaced with a per-bet null: each bet's true win probability is the de-vigged closing market probability of the side taken, which is the sharpest honest benchmark. The three-leg parlay moved from p = 0.0005 to p = 0.218. Both the old and new numbers are recorded here rather than quietly overwritten — the correction is part of the evidence.

Second correction: a sign error in the Week 11 card

The first build of the fair-lines table computed the gap between the model and the market as fair_margin + spread_line. That is wrong. The archive's spread_line is the home handicap — a positive number means the home club is favoured — so the gap is a subtraction. Adding the two produced absurd gaps: 18.6 points on IND @ HOU and 29.6 on MIA @ BUF, on a model whose own mean absolute error on the home margin is 10.5 points.

The convention is now tested in the pipeline rather than asserted in a comment, on all 7,413 graded rows: regressing the actual home margin on the archive number gives a slope of 1.045 and an intercept of -0.04, and the home club covers (margin − line) > 0 on 47.6% of games. A priced market has to land near 50%; the mirrored form, (margin + line) > 0, lands on 59.2%, which is the tell. Both numbers are printed on the Fair lines page so anyone can repeat the check by eye.

Corrected, the largest disagreement on the Week 11 card is 4.2 points and most are under three — inside the model's own error. That is a far less exciting result, and it is the true one. The backtest engine already had the convention right (it subtracts the line in outcome_spread), so the leaderboard, the ROI figures and the luck tests are unaffected; only the Week 11 origination card was wrong.

The eight validation gates

  1. Provenance — every input is in the source registry with an access route.
  2. Coverage — the strategy produced priced, graded bets.
  3. Baseline — the comparison is the de-vigged closing price, not a naive guess.
  4. Leakage — every input predates kickoff.
  5. Out-of-sample size — at least 300 graded bets, pre-declared.
  6. Robustness — positive in at least half of the seasons.
  7. Prospective trial — frozen version, predictions logged before outcomes.
  8. Release review — human sign-off. Never automated.

Gates 7 and 8 cannot be self-certified by this lab, which is why nothing is reward-eligible.

python3 scripts/nfl_lines/stage5_injuries.py     # tracker + impact

python3 scripts/nfl_lines/stage7_decision.py     # questions + suggestions
python3 scripts/nfl_lines/sources.py             # source registry
python3 scripts/nfl_lines/stage8_site.py         # this site
python3 scripts/nfl_lines/verify.py              # build the verification report

The random seed is fixed, so re-running produces byte-identical numbers unless an input file changes.
