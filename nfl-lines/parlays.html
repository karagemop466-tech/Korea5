Parlays and prop combos — correlation-aware

Multiplying leg probabilities assumes the legs are independent. They often are not: two props on the same team share a game script, and a game total and a quarterback's passing yards move together. This page measures those relationships first, then prices the combination with a Gaussian copula that consumes the measured matrix.

No payout, no ROI, no stake. There is no parlay price and no prop price in any reachable source. What follows is a joint probability. Turning that into a wager needs a price, and the price is missing — recorded as GAP-1 and GAP-2.

Single-bet vs parlay vs prop combo

Single

One probability, one price. The only market where a conventional edge can even be defined. Tested in the leaderboard as a probability, since the price is absent.

Parlay (cross-game)

Legs come from different games, so they are near-independent by construction. The real risk is not correlation but the compounded vig — which cannot be computed without prices.

Prop combo (same-game)

Legs share a game script. Correlation is the whole question, and it is measurable from outcomes. Measured values are on the right.

How the joint probability is computed

  1. Marginals come from the model, never from a guess.
  2. Each correlated pair contributes its measured r to a correlation matrix.
  3. A Cholesky factor of that matrix drives a Gaussian copula; the joint probability is the share of simulated draws in which every leg wins.
  4. If the matrix is not positive definite (which happens with noisy estimates), the code falls back to independence and says so in its output rather than silently producing a number.
