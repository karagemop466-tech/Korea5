How a player becomes a number

Each reported player contributes status credit (how unavailable they look) times a position weight (how much their absence is worth). These two tables are the only discretionary inputs in the model, so the whole ranking is re-run at ×0.5 and ×2.0 scaling of the weights and both results are published.

What the model does with a deficit

  • The difference between two clubs' deficits shifts the expected home margin in points, and that shift moves the win probability through the same normal model as everything else — there is no separate injury “edge” term.
  • It is a weighted sum, not a causal estimate. The site claims no measured injury-to-performance coefficient, because measuring one needs seasons of player-level snaps, which the official pages here do not expose.
  • Inactive is not injured. The 2026 rulebook sets a 48-player game-day active roster (47 with fewer than eight primary offensive linemen), a 53-man axis, elevation to 54/55 only via the Standard Elevation Addendum, and a 16/17-player practice squad — so a healthy player can be inactive. The model uses only what the official report states.
  • Practice status and game status are independent fields (one published row reads “Knee, Limited in practice, Out”); both are stored, and neither is derived from the other.

Still not computable from official data here

  • Replacement quality — there is no official depth-chart page (nfl.com/depth-charts/ and the per-club depth-chart paths return 404), so second- and third-string tiers cannot be asserted and are reported as a gap wherever they would be used.
  • Snap-weighted exposure — needs play-by-play or participation data the capture does not hold.
  • Dated availability history — needs one archive written per week before kick-off; the forward ledger starts accumulating exactly that.
