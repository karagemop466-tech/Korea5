How a fair price becomes a posted price

  • Win probability p → fair American odds: -100·p/(1-p) when p ≥ 0.5, +100·(1-p)/p below it. No vig and no market information in that step.
  • Fair decimal → posted decimal: each side's implied probability is multiplied by 1 + hold/2, so the two sides sum above 1. The hold used here is the conventional 4.5%, shown to make the mechanics visible — it is not an observation about any book.
  • Totals are the model's expected combined score; the uncertainty used is the fitted margin spread (11.25 points). A real total would also need venue, weather and pace inputs this capture does not hold.
  • Shrinkage is applied before pricing, with the same λ (0.79) that the backtest validated, so a line cannot be sharper than the forecast behind it.

How far to trust a spread like this These margins come from eight-to-ten games of 2026 ratings, blended over the shrink values the sample cannot distinguish. A line beyond about ten points is the model over-reaching rather than a market view, and a total is centred on the league average because nothing in this capture describes venue, weather or pace. They are useful as a forecast to be scored; they are not a price to act on, and they are not offered as one.
