NFL Lines & Strategy Lab

A disciplined framework for originating independent prices, evaluating single bets and correlated combinations, and rewarding repeatable predictive skill rather than short-run outcomes.

Current state: no strategy has been backtested here. This repository does not currently contain timestamped historical market odds, player-prop prices, official play-by-play model inputs, or a validated injury-to-performance dataset. No edge, fair line, correlation coefficient, ROI, or leaderboard winner is claimed. Production leaderboard and line generation remain blocked until the required data are licensed/available and all gates pass.

Executive summary

  • Separate forecasts from prices: first estimate outcome probabilities, then convert to fair odds and compare with an archived, timestamped offered price after vig.
  • Use chronological, rolling out-of-sample validation, a final untouched holdout, uncertainty intervals, and closing-line comparison; never select on raw win rate.
  • Model injuries by player, position, expected role and replacement tier—not a binary team label. Confirm official availability and actual participation separately; do not infer “second/third/fourth string” absent a sourced depth chart and dated status.
  • Estimate parlay joint probabilities directly or from a calibrated joint model. Multiplying marginal probabilities is invalid when legs are dependent.
  • Start with transparent baselines (market-implied, league-average, team/player historical) and require each new strategy to beat them out of sample after costs.

Correlation: measure it; do not invent it

For each pair of legs, store the exact market/line and estimate association from historical joint outcomes using only data available before the forecast timestamp. Report sample size, uncertainty interval, season range and conditional context. Pearson correlation of binary outcomes is a descriptive phi coefficient, not a universal parlay pricing adjustment. Use conditional/joint probabilities for payout valuation. Shrink noisy estimates toward zero or a pooled prior selected using training data only; publish the unshrunk estimate and uncertainty too. If there is not enough evidence, display unverified / blank, not zero.

Validation gates (must pass in order)

  1. Data provenance: every feature/outcome has source, event ID, timestamp, definition and revision history; point-in-time snapshots avoid future information.
  2. Coverage and integrity: reconcile gamebooks/official stats; quantify missingness and late corrections; no silent imputation of unverified injury/role values.
  3. Baseline: beat simple league/team/player baselines and a properly de-vigged market reference on identical eligible events.
  4. Leakage audit: features frozen to decision time; split by time, not random row; preprocessing and feature selection fitted within training folds.
  5. Out-of-sample performance: report proper probability scores, calibration, line error, ROI only with exact historical prices and settlement rules, uncertainty intervals and sample size.
  6. Robustness: performance across seasons, market types and injury certainty strata; sensitivity to vig, stale quotes, pushes/voids and multiple testing.
  7. Prospective paper trial: freeze strategy/version and log predictions before outcomes for a predeclared evaluation horizon. No leaderboard prize until complete.
  8. Release review: reproducible run, independent review, no disqualifying integrity or responsible-use concerns; retain failed candidates in audit logs.

Leaderboard design proposal

Initially paper-only, versioned strategy entries, immutable timestamped forecasts, fixed eligible market set, and public methodology. Rank by predeclared out-of-sample proper score improvement over the same market baseline, with calibration and minimum-evidence gates; display uncertainty and coverage beside rank. Do not rank by winning streak, raw accuracy, or unadjusted ROI. Reserve any reward decision until legal, privacy, fairness, responsible-play, and anti-manipulation review. A strategy that fails any gate is “not qualified,” not silently removed.

Injury and lineup tracking requirements

Suggested schema: game/player identifiers; position; official status as published; report publication time; practice participation; game-day inactive confirmation; transaction/roster event; role/depth-chart evidence URL and timestamp; replacement identity/tier only when an official source supports it; expected snap/opportunity share (model estimate clearly labelled); actual participation from official gamebook; source revision and capture time. Track offense and defense symmetrically, including OL combinations, QB, skill positions, defensive front and secondary. A coach's starter announcement is not the same as official active status or actual playing time. Missing values stay blank and flagged.
