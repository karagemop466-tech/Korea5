Method

Validation gates

A strategy is eligible only if it passes every gate. Default gates: (1) minimum out-of-sample sample size; (2) no look-ahead bias check; (3) model edge versus market above a threshold; (4) minimum selectivity (the strategy does not bet every game); (5) statistical significance on the realised edge. Gates are configurable and each strategy records its own gate results.

Source: —

Correlation method

Correlation between selections is estimated from official play-level data (Next Gen Stats, S5). For multi-leg parlays, the combined probability is recomputed with the correlation matrix rather than assuming independence. Same-game parlays model positive dependence explicitly. Empirical correlation values are computed and published once the data feed is live; until then the values are blank and flagged.

Source: S5 · NFL Next Gen Stats

Removing luck (skill vs luck)

Luck is removed by (1) testing on out-of-sample data only, (2) ranking on edge versus the market / closing-line value rather than raw hit rate, (3) requiring minimum selectivity so high-volume variance is not rewarded, (4) applying validation gates before eligibility, and (5) reporting risk-adjusted, correlation-adjusted returns. A strategy is treated as skill-based only after it clears the gates out-of-sample.

Source: —

Ranking metric

Leaderboard ranking uses a risk-adjusted, luck-adjusted metric: correlation-adjusted edge versus market, normalised by exposure, with a selectivity penalty for unselective strategies. Raw hit rate is never the primary rank.

Source: —

Line origination

Lines are model-implied probabilities produced by our own model from official inputs (stats, play-level data, schedule, injuries). They are labelled model-originated and are not sourced from any bookmaker feed. No bookmaker odds are scraped or presented as source data.

Source: S4 · NFL Player Stats

Injury and roster signal model

Each team's offense and defense depth is tracked by position string (1st-string starter, 2nd/3rd/4th-string backups). When a backup is elevated to starter due to injury, the model records the position, the player, and the expected signal impact. The official weekly injury report (S2) supplies injury and game status; depth charts are confirmed against official team/league sources and third-party aggregators are used only as leads, never as verified values.

Source: S2 · NFL Injury Report

Honesty rules

Every table row links to an official source. A value that cannot be confirmed from an official source is left blank and flagged as unverified. Values are never inferred or filled in. Model outputs are labelled as model-originated, not as sourced market data.

Source: —

Leaderboard

Risk-adjusted, luck-adjusted correlation-adjusted edge vs market (closing-line value), selectivity-penalised. Raw hit rate is not the primary rank. Eligibility: A strategy is eligible only if it has passed every validation gate (M1) and meets the minimum out-of-sample sample size.. See the Leaderboard page for reward tiers.

How the three NFL surfaces relate. This hub holds the structured layer: the requirements list, the method and validation-gate reference, the leaderboard reward design and the source registry. The NFL Lines Lab holds the evidence layer, built only on official NFL records — the published injury report, official player game logs and the official Game Book — with every correlation and minimum-sample figure computed from them. The NFL Lines Lab (model) holds the model layer: a line model built and back-tested against third-party game data, with the market spread left blank because its upstream source is not named. None of the three publishes a validated edge, and each says so on its own landing page.

Responsible gaming: this is decision-support research, not a wagering service. No real-money wagers are placed. Betting carries risk; please gamble responsibly.
