Lines &amp; market

How a line would be originated from official data, what de-vigging does to a price, and why no market line is published here: the official NFL sources publish no odds at all.

What we do not have

  • No price feed. The official NFL week pages, game pages and stats pages were probed by exact regex for odds, spread, moneyline and totals fields: zero hits on every page (nfl/research/probe.json).
  • Therefore: no market line, no ROI, no closing-line value, no certified bet.
  • The arithmetic below is a tool demonstrated on stated example prices. It is not a market and it is not advice.

How a line would be originated — the working method

  1. Estimate each side's scoring. Own shrunk points-for against the opponent's shrunk points-against, re-centred on the league mean. Shrunk means n/(n+9), so an 11-game sample carries about 55% weight and a hot start cannot masquerade as a season truth.
  2. Convert to a margin and a total. Margin = home estimate − away estimate; total = sum.
  3. Convert to a probability. Nothing on this site does this yet, because doing it properly requires an outcome distribution calibrated on many seasons, and this system has two partial ones. Publishing a probability now would be a guess wearing a decimal point.
  4. Compare with a market price. Impossible: there is no market price. This is the step that makes a line a line, and it is the one step the official data cannot supply.

De-vig: turning a two-way price into a fair price

A two-way price hides a margin. Removing it changes the fair probability, and how you remove it changes the answer. Both standard methods are shown, with the gap between them, so the choice is visible rather than buried.

Why parlays cost more than they look

Each leg carries the same per-leg margin, so the margin compounds. At a −110/−110 per-leg price, a five-leg parlay carries roughly five times the effective hold of a single. That is arithmetic, not opinion.
