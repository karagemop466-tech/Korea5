Q01 Our own lines Which official source publishes NFL point spreads, moneylines or totals that we are allowed to cite row by row? Nothing. nfl.com game pages, week pages and team stat pages were probed by exact regex (nfl/research/probe.json): odds/spread/moneyline field counts are all 0. No official NFL price feed is available in this repository. Any price must therefore be typed in by the owner with the book, timestamp and screenshot, or the system publishes a model number and labels it MODEL — not a market line. Decide whether to consume a licensed odds API under the owner's own account, or to record prices manually as observations. Both need a decision before any ROI claim is published. Every ROI, closing-line-value and price-outperformance claim. open official
www.nfl.com
Q02 Our own lines What does 'originating my own line' mean for this project: a fair-price model, or a tradable two-way price with a hold? The engine can produce win probabilities and devigged fair prices from any two-way price pair (functions implemented in nfl/tools/analyze_market.py and unit-tested). The owner has not stated whether the published number must include a margin (juice) and, if so, whether it should be a fixed hold or a per-market hold. One decision from the owner. Default proposed in nfl/curated/validation-gates.json: publish fair price and a suggested price at a stated hold. Presentation of the line, and the definition of a winning bet. open official
www.nfl.com
Q03 Skill vs luck How many settled bets are needed before a strategy may be called skill-based rather than lucky? A statistical gate is implemented (binomial test against the break-even probability implied by the price, plus a Monte-Carlo shuffle test that destroys any real edge). The threshold is a policy choice, not a fact. The gate currently refuses to certify anything below 100 settled bets and reports the p-value so the owner can see how weak the evidence is. Owner decision on the false-positive budget. Record it in nfl/curated/validation-gates.json before results accumulate, so the threshold cannot be moved after the fact. Leaderboard certification and rewards. partial official
www.nfl.com
Q04 Skill vs luck Are the rewards real money, an honour system, or a paper scoreboard? A leaderboard schema with scoring formulas (Brier score, log loss, ROI, closing-line value, calibration error) and a demotion/probation rule. No reward currency, no payout rule and no eligibility rule exists. The site therefore publishes ranks only and states that no monetary reward is promised. Owner decision. Until then the leaderboard is explicitly 'no reward, ranking only'. Any statement that a strategy 'earns' something. open official
www.nfl.com
Q05 Injuries Does the official injury report tell us who will actually start? The NFL official report gives player, position, injury, practice status and game status (Out / Doubtful / Questionable) per team per week. 2026 weeks 1-10 and 2025 weeks 1-18 are loaded. It does not name the replacement. 'Questionable' is not a probability. Practice status is not a snap count. Join the injury report to each club's official depth chart (done) and to official snap-count/gamebook data (not yet loaded; see Q07). Any 'defence loses its 2nd-string corner' claim. partial official
www.nfl.com
Q06 Injuries How deep is each defence and offence behind the listed starter — is the 2nd string a real drop-off or a coin flip? Official club depth charts give 1st/2nd/3rd/4th string at every position for each club that publishes one, with the club's own 'updated as of' date. A depth chart is a projection by the club, not a performance measurement. Several clubs label theirs 'Unofficial Depth Chart'. Depth is not quality: the 2nd string is not always worse. Replace the projection with measured performance of the actual participants (official snap counts and player stats). Until then the site shows depth as a flag, never as a rating. Quantifying 'deficiency' in a defence or offence. partial official
www.49ers.com
Q07 Injuries Which players actually played, and how many snaps? Nothing official in this repository yet. Player season stat pages publish per-game logs (WK, OPP, RESULT and stat columns) which imply participation. No official snap-count feed is loaded. Absence from a game log is not proof of zero snaps — a player can play and record no stat. Add the official NFL gamebook / snap-count publication as a source, or accept that participation is inferred from stat logs and label it as such. Backup-vs-starter strength measurement. open official
www.nfl.com
Q08 Correlation Which player props in the same game are positively correlated, and by how much? Official per-game player stat logs for the loaded seasons, which is exactly the input a correlation estimate needs. The sample is small (a partial season), so any correlation estimate carries a wide confidence interval. Estimates are published with n and a bootstrap interval, and are withheld below n=8 games. Accumulate more official game logs; resample with a block bootstrap; never publish a correlation without its interval. Parlay correlation adjustment and same-game parlay pricing. partial official
www.nfl.com
Q09 Correlation Do game-level outcomes correlate with the props inside them (e.g. a big favourite rushing more)? Final scores for every played 2025 and 2026 game, plus per-game player logs. Mapping a player log row to a specific game requires the opponent and week to match the schedule; that join is implemented but only covers games where both official pages exist. Complete the join and report the match rate. Unmatched rows are excluded and counted, never guessed. Cross-market correlation (spread vs rushing prop). open official
www.nfl.com
Q10 Validation What is the minimum sample and the multiple-testing correction before a strategy is promoted? A gate list with explicit pass/fail logic, including a Benjamini-Hochberg style false-discovery guard when many strategies are tested at once. The reward rule and the promotion threshold are policy, not fact. Defaults are proposed and marked as defaults. Owner sign-off, recorded before results are seen. Publishing 'top performing strategy'. open official
www.nfl.com
Q11 Validation Is there enough history to backtest at all? 2025 and 2026 official results and player logs are loaded on demand by CI. Two partial seasons with no historical prices is not a backtest of a betting strategy; it is a backtest of a probability model. Any strategy that needs prices cannot be backtested yet (see Q01). Either obtain licensed historical prices, or restrict backtests to probability-scoring (Brier/log-loss) and label them 'no price backtest'. Any historical ROI claim. blocked official
www.nfl.com
Q12 Data quality Are the depth charts the same thing across all 32 clubs? Per-club pages with per-club 'updated as of' dates and per-club naming (some say 'Unofficial'). They are not normalised: some clubs publish 4 tiers, some 3; some list 11 defensive positions, some 12. Cross-club comparison of 'depth' is therefore approximate and is flagged as such. Publish a per-club chart schema note and never sum across clubs without saying so. League-wide 'most injured defence' style claims. partial official
www.chiefs.com
Q13 Data quality Could a page change between two fetches in the same day? Every record carries the fetch time and the exact source URL it came from. Yes — injury reports and depth charts change during the week, and week pages gain scores. The site therefore stamps each table with its retrieval time. Re-run the CI fetch before publishing anything time-sensitive; the site shows the stamp so staleness is visible. Nothing, provided the stamp is shown. answered official
www.nfl.com
Q14 Data quality How do the two partial-season datasets compare in size and shape? Counts per dataset are published on the Data health page and in nfl/data/fetch-report.json. Coverage is not completeness. A missing week page means missing games, not zero games; the report lists every failed fetch. Read fetch-report.json failures before trusting any aggregate. Nothing, provided failures stay visible. answered official
www.nfl.com
Q15 Model What is the baseline every strategy must beat? Baselines defined: coin flip (50% ATS), home-field prior from official results, and the market's own devigged price when a price exists. With no prices (Q01), the only baselines available are probability-scoring baselines, not ROI baselines. State the baseline per competition in the strategy registry. Any 'beats the baseline' claim. partial official
www.nfl.com
Q16 Model How should rest, travel and short weeks be handled? Official kickoff dates and venues for every 2026 game, from which rest days can be derived exactly. Travel distance is not published; deriving it means adding a venue-coordinate source, which is not an official NFL page. Either drop travel, or publish it as an estimate with the non-official coordinate source named. Travel-based signals. open official
www.nfl.com
Q17 Governance Who reviews a suggestion, and what stops a suggestion contradicting a verified table? A suggestions register with status, owner, review gate and a check that a suggestion touching a verified row must cite its source. No reviewer is assigned. The register therefore defaults every row to 'awaiting review' and nothing is auto-accepted. Assign the reviewer role. Until then the Suggestion review page lists decisions in open state only. Any suggestion being marked 'chosen'. open official
github.com
Q18 Governance What happens when two strategies claim the same edge, or when a strategy's live results diverge from its backtest? A probation rule: a strategy whose live scoring diverges from its declared expectation by more than the stated tolerance is demoted to probation and its reward is withheld. The tolerance value is a policy choice, not a fact. Owner sets the tolerance in nfl/curated/validation-gates.json. Reward allocation. open official
