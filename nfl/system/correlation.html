Correlation

What correlation values will be computed from, why none is published yet, and the rules that forbid multiplying leg probabilities.

No real correlation value is published on this page yet. The ingested official player-stat feed contains season-to-date leaders, not per-game logs joined by team and week. A correlation computed from season totals would be a correlation of 32 aggregate points, not of game outcomes. Refusing to publish it is the honest output; see Q08 and the method page.

What is needed: Per-player game logs (WK / OPP / stat columns), which nfl.com publishes on each player page. That ingest is the next task and is not claimed as done here.

Why a fake correlation would be worse than none

A correlation is a claim that two things move together. Computing it from season totals across 32 clubs — which is what the ingested leader boards contain — answers a completely different question: it would tell you whether good teams have good players, which is trivially true and useless for a parlay. The number would look precise and mean nothing.

So the page shows the machinery on clearly-labelled synthetic data, and withholds the real value until the real per-game logs are ingested. That is a deliberate refusal, not an oversight.

How the real value will be computed (and why it needs a join)

  1. Fetch each player's official game log (WK, OPP, RESULT, stat columns) from their nfl.com player page.
  2. Join two players by club and week — not by season total — so each observation is one shared game.
  3. Compute Pearson r, then a 1,000-draw bootstrap for the 90% interval.
  4. If the interval spans zero, publish the literal words "indistinguishable from zero". With 8 games, that will be most pairs.
  5. Never multiply leg probabilities for a same-player or same-game combo. Publish the empirical joint, the independence product, and the signed difference.

Combination rules that already bind

  • C1. Never multiply leg probabilities unless the legs are on different games, different teams and different players, and even then publish the empirical joint as the primary number.
  • C2. A parlay's price must show the hold compounding: a three-leg parlay at the same hold has roughly three times the margin of a single.
  • C3. Correlation between legs must be reported with its interval, and a correlation whose interval spans zero must be printed as 'indistinguishable from zero'.
  • C4. A player-prop combo on one player (e.g. rushing yards over AND receptions over) is a single joint event and must be estimated from that player's joint outcomes, never from two marginals.
