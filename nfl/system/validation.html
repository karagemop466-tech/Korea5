How luck is removed — the actual mechanism

1. Proper scoring. Brier and log loss cannot be gamed by hedging or by refusing to commit. A win rate can: go 12–0 on coin flips and you look brilliant. Score those same coin flips and you do not.

2. Shuffle test. Take the strategy's picks, keep them, and randomly permute the outcomes 2,000 times. If the real score sits inside that shuffled distribution, the result is what a know-nothing would have got. The test on a home-field prior currently returns p = 1.000 — inside the distribution, so: luck.

3. Minimum sample. A 60% win rate on 20 bets is noise. The gate will not look at a strategy below 100 settled bets, and it prints that refusal rather than a provisional verdict.

4. Calibration. A model that says 60% must be right 60% of the time, not 45% of the time while winning 60% of its picks. Expected calibration error is computed over deciles.

5. Multiple testing. Testing 40 strategies guarantees a winner. The false-discovery guard raises the bar as the number of entrants grows, so the winner must be surprising given how hard we looked.

6. Point-in-time integrity. Information published after the decision cutoff is a leak, not a signal. Every input carries a publication time; the cutoff is kickoff minus 60 minutes, which is safely after the NFL's final game-status update at 90 minutes before kickoff.
