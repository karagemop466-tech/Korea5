Vacation windows, 2027–2029

Two lenses, both computed from the event feed. Radio counts only games that are actually broadcast (Giants, Athletics, 49ers, Stanford, Cal, Westwood One national). All games also counts every other MLB and NFL row in the scope, including the NFL all-star exhibition.

Radio lens: 13 calendar days in 2027. All-games lens: 6 calendar days. Lengths below count inclusive calendar days, not hotel nights. On the strictest reading — every MLB game in the league, every NFL game, and the Pro Bowl exhibition — no 7-, 14- or 21-day window exists in 2027–2029; the longest clean runs are 6, 6 and 6 days. Drop the exhibition, which is not a league game and is not on Westwood One's published schedule, and a 13-day February window opens in every one of the three years.

The answer: the conference-championship → Super Bowl gap

Book Feb 1 – Feb 13, 2027. It is the longest run of consecutive calendar days in 2027 on which nothing in your list is on the air: no MLB, no NFL, no Stanford, no Cal, no Westwood One national window. It sits between conference-championship Sunday and Super Bowl LXI, and 2027 is the only one of the three years where both of those anchors are pinned to official dates.

Why not the summer? MLB plays a slate almost every day from late February to early October, and your scope counts every MLB game, not just the Giants and Athletics. So April–September is busy on nearly every day by definition. The winter gap is the only place a multi-week trip fits.

Strict all-games view (counts the NFL all-star exhibition)

If you also refuse to travel during the Pro Bowl Games, the longest fully free run drops to 6 calendar days in each year, and it splits the February window in two. The Pro Bowl is an all-star exhibition rather than a league game, and Westwood One's published 2026-27 NFL schedule does not list it, so it was removed from the radio-only scope in this revision and is still counted in the all-games scope. That is the only difference between the two answers.
